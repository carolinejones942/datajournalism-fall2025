#### The data

The data analysis in this story started from the U.S. Forest Service data, which weighed risk factors such as precipitation and previous fires, and gave land spaces across the U.S., 18 acres each, a wildfire risk score on a 1 to 5 scale. Using that data, this analysis narrowed the scope to about 5,000 small communities in 11 states. This analysis also added a one-mile buffer zone to each community that represents the potential spread of fire embers. Then, this analysis gave each small community its own wildfire hazard score on a 1 to 5 scale. Furthermore, unlike the data from the U.S. Forest Service, this analysis accounted for other common aspects that increase fire hazard risks, such as evacuation constraints, age, disabilities, alerting, and mobile homes. The findings were that Paradise, California had a score of 3.81, and 526 communities had a higher score than Paradise. 

I believe the data analysis in this story is very strong. Nothing is lacking. That said, I am sure that other factors not listed in the story could play into fire hazards. The story mentioned that mobile homes are built with more materials that are combustible. What about other buildings in the town? Additionally, age, disability, and escape routes were all mentioned in the story, but what about people who could leave, but just refuse to leave? What is the prevalence of that, if any? Again, these components are not necessary for the story; they are just ways the already-strong analysis could go deeper.

The rest of the story does not taper off like an inverted pyramid, for example; instead, it is equally important throughout the piece, to the end. The Martini Glass structure works well here because this piece needed to have a thorough discussion of each aspect that affects fire hazards, including evacuation constraints, age, disabilities, alerting, and mobile homes.
